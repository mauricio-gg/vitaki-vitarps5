VitaRPS5: four UI directions

Issue #271, round 2. Four finished-looking mocks at the Vita's native 960×544. Keys: arrows = D-pad, Enter = Cross, Esc = Circle, T = Triangle, Q/E = L/R, Space = Start; clicks act as touch. Each has a View on device toggle that frames the screen in a generic handheld, and covers Home, Settings, Connecting (8 steps), the in-stream overlay, network unstable, Connect via, Register PIN, the error popup, and ready / standby / not registered / PSN-only consoles. Game covers are fictional, generated art. Everything is drawable with vita2d; see FEASIBILITY.md.

CEO's pick so far
Direction 1

XMB “Remote Play Bar”

A row of categories over a vertical list, on silk ribbons tinted by the time of day. Settings are items with a value panel that previews each choice, and Triangle slides in an options panel. This round adds ribbon depth and dust, a custom duotone icon set, real cover art, staggered transitions, and a compressed trail so categories left of the selected one stay on screen and tappable.

Pros

  • Most controller-native: left/right category, up/down item.
  • One pattern covers all five areas; new screens are cheap.
  • Ribbons are about 12 draw calls and no big texture.
  • Settings panel shows what each option means.

Cons

  • The running game is a small thumbnail, no hero moment.
  • Fifteen settings in one vertical list.
  • Home is about 95 draw calls as drawn, over the 80 target until trimmed.
  • Trail of small icons on the left is our own variation, not true XMB.
Home about 95 draw calls (about 78 trimmed) · about 5 MB textures

Direction 2

LiveArea Modern

The Vita's own idea, grown up. Consoles are glass bubbles in a honeycomb on a soft wallpaper, with a ring that shows state. Opening one peels into its own gate page: a big Start bubble, the game cover, and floating frames for route and last session. Tools (Settings, Controller, Profile, Logs) sit on a second page. Round bubbles also drive the stream overlay.

Pros

  • Feels native to the device and to Vita owners.
  • Great touch: big round targets, page peel to go back.
  • Gate page uses the cover and shows state at a glance.
  • Friendly and warm; easy to extend with more bubbles.

Cons

  • Two levels: open the gate, then Start. One more press to play.
  • Needs a wallpaper texture (about 2 MB) and glass bubbles baked per state.
  • The circular reveal becomes a scaled disc and a fade, not a true clip.
  • Bubbles hold few details, so busy consoles need the gate page.
Home about 56 draw calls, gate about 65 · about 8 MB textures

Direction 3

Editorial

Type first. A paper-coloured screen, black ink, one orange accent, and the selected console name set at 104 px, with the rest of the list in a quiet index. Motion is in the type: names rise and swap, and in Settings the giant group title changes as you scroll. Selection is an inverted bar. Almost no images are needed, so it works with or without covers.

Pros

  • Cheapest to draw: rectangles and text, about 48 calls.
  • Best legibility on a 5 inch screen; the most distinctive look.
  • No dependency on covers or console art quality.
  • Quick to build and easy to localise.

Cons

  • A light theme uses more power on an OLED Vita and can feel bright in a dark room.
  • Least game-like; the cover is small and decorative.
  • Text rows are smaller touch targets than tiles or bubbles.
  • Needs a 104 px glyph set (small atlas, but another size to load).

Direction 4

Cover-flow

A spatial shelf. Each console is a game cover in a perspective carousel with a spotlight, floor grid and reflections; side cards turn away, the focused one steps forward. Settings are a rotating drum of rows next to a tilted detail card, and Connecting stacks the eight steps in depth. It puts the game art first.

Pros

  • Shows game art best; the most premium and the biggest wow.
  • Depth makes focus obvious without extra chrome.
  • The drum and step stack keep the spatial idea through every screen.
  • Works well with few consoles.

Cons

  • Highest build risk: vita2d has no perspective textured quad, so side cards need warped textures baked at load.
  • About 7 MB of textures with the baked angles and reflections.
  • Leans on cover art; fallback cards look plainer.
  • The drum list is the least efficient for 15 settings.
Home about 70 draw calls · about 7 MB textures

Designer's recommendation. Keep XMB as the base, since it is your pick and the cheapest to get right. If you want one more ingredient, take LiveArea's gate page for a console (big Start button, cover, last session) as the screen that opens when you press Cross on a console, then keep XMB for everything else. Editorial is the best choice if build time and legibility matter most; Cover-flow is the one to pick only if you accept the perspective-texture risk for the sake of the wow.
Game covers and the stream scene are AI-generated fictional art with no marks. Console renders are generic placeholders. Blur and glow in the mocks become pre-blurred textures and 9-slice panels on the Vita. Each direction has its own token file (tokens-*.css). Open files directly in a browser; no build step. Fonts load from Google Fonts.
